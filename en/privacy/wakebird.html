Privacy Policy — Wakebird

Last updated 9 October 2026 · applies to Wakebird for iOS

Wakebird keeps your alarms on your iPhone. Webspind runs no server and cannot see when you wake up or which chorus you chose. The only thing sent on is purchase details. This page explains it in detail, and exists because Apple requires a privacy policy for every app.

What the app stores

The alarms you set (time, days and chorus) and whether you finished the introduction. They stay on your iPhone and are scheduled with iOS's own alarm system. The widget reads a small copy of your alarms from a shared space on the same iPhone. There is no account, no advertising identifier, no fingerprinting and no analytics.

Permissions

Wakebird asks for permission to set alarms, and uses it only for the alarms you set and the one-minute test alarm you ask for. Nothing else: no location, no microphone, no contacts, no health data.

Siri and Shortcuts

If you ask Siri to set an alarm, the request is handled by Apple and the alarm is set on your iPhone. Webspind receives nothing.

Purchases

Purchases are handled by Apple. To confirm and restore them, the app uses RevenueCat, which receives an anonymous app user ID, your purchase details (product, price, time and App Store receipt) and technical details such as app version and country. RevenueCat receives no alarms, name or email.

Crash reports

If you share analytics with developers in your iPhone settings, Apple may send me aggregated crash reports with device model, iOS version and a stack trace. They contain no name, email or alarms.

Children

Wakebird is not directed at children.

Delete your data

Deleting the app deletes everything it stored and every alarm it set. For your purchase data at RevenueCat, write to me and I will have it deleted.

Changes

If the policy changes in a way that matters, the date at the top changes and the change is mentioned in the app's release notes.

Questions, or want something deleted? Write to webspind@gmail.com and I will answer within two working days.
