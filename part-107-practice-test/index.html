What the real test looks like

The Part 107 knowledge test is the FAA's Unmanned Aircraft General (UAG) test: 60 questions, 2 hours, and a passing score of 70, according to the FAA Airman Knowledge Testing Matrix of 25 September 2026. The same document says you need an FAA Tracking Number from IACRA before you test.

The questions here follow the five areas of the FAA's Airman Certification Standards for small UAS (FAA-S-ACS-10B): regulations, airspace, weather, loading and performance, and operations. The regulations were last checked on 7 November 2026.

How to use it

Answer first, then read the explanation, even when you got it right. The reason behind an answer is what carries over when the real test asks the same rule a different way. Every answer names the regulation or FAA handbook it comes from, so you can look it up.

Wrong on a question? Good: that is the topic to read next. Run the test again later and see whether it stuck.

Questions people ask

Are these the real FAA test questions?

No. They were written for the DroneCamp app from the FAA's Airman Certification Standards and the regulations, and each answer cites its source. DroneCamp and Webspind are not affiliated with the FAA.

How many questions are on the real Part 107 test?

The FAA's testing matrix lists the Unmanned Aircraft General test (UAG) at 60 questions, 2 hours and a passing score of 70.

Does a good score here mean I will pass?

No. These 40 questions are a sample, so your score tells you what to study next, not how the real test will go.

Do you keep my answers?

No. The test runs in your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere or saved, and the page has no tracking, cookies or ads.
